Feature selection — daily independent variables

Four families. “In demo model” = implemented from the technical block or from the synthetic exogenous columns; everything else is a recommended data feed to add as extra CSV columns (unknown columns are auto-featurised as 5-day changes).

Selection rules

  • Point-in-time only: every variable must be knowable at the close of day t (publication-time stamped). Monthly/quarterly series enter as event flags or step functions with their real release dates, never back-filled to the reference period.
  • Parsimony: ≤ 20 features per ~500 training days; ridge penalty; re-select quarterly using out-of-sample (not in-sample) information gain.
  • Economic prior first: each variable needs a written transmission mechanism and expected sign; unexpected sign flips are flagged for review rather than accepted.

Bull / Bear regime classifier

1 · Label (what “Bear” means)

σ̂ₜ  = stdev( rₜ₋₅₉ … rₜ ),   rₜ = ln(Pₜ / Pₜ₋₁)
Fwdₜ = ln(Pₜ₊ₕ / Pₜ)
yₜ  = 1  if  Fwdₜ < −k · σ̂ₜ · √H      (Bear)
       0  otherwise                     (Bull)

2 · Probability model (ridge-logistic)

xₜ,ⱼ = clip( (fₜ,ⱼ − μⱼ) / sⱼ , ±5 )     μ, s from TRAIN rows only
zₜ   = b + Σⱼ wⱼ · xₜ,ⱼ
P(Bear)ₜ = 1 / (1 + e^(−zₜ))
(b,w) = argmin −Σ [ y·ln p + (1−y)·ln(1−p) ] + λ‖w‖²     (λ = 10, b unpenalised)

3 · Decision rule (cost-based threshold)

θ = c_FA / (c_FA + c_MISS)          (Bayes-optimal cut on a calibrated P)
Enter Bear : P(Bear)ₜ ≥ θ  for N consecutive closes
Exit  Bear : P(Bear)ₜ < θ − δ
Regimeₜ = Bear (1) / Bull (0);   position at t+1 = 0 if Bear else 1

4 · Risk-management rationale

  • Asymmetric loss. Missing a drawdown costs more than a false alarm that merely sidesteps some upside, so θ < 0.5. With miss:false-alarm = 2:1, θ = 1/3. The ratio is a portfolio-level input (set from your drawdown budget), not a statistic.
  • Hysteresis (δ) + confirmation (N). Probabilities are noisy; a gap between entry and exit levels and a multi-day confirm cut whipsaw, which is expensive at ~25 bp round-trip friction on a Thai small/mid-cap.
  • Calibration check. The Bayes cut is only valid if P is calibrated; the reliability table (right) must lie near the diagonal. If not, recalibrate (Platt/isotonic on a separate validation block) before trusting θ.
  • Label scale. Volatility-scaled threshold keeps “Bear” meaning the same in calm and stormy markets.

Binary output is a simplification; always read the probability and its drivers alongside the label.

1 · Business problem statement

Why daily COM7 price-driver analytics matter. COM7 is Thailand’s largest IT-retail chain (1,323 locations at Mar-2026) and a distribution proxy for Apple’s Thai demand; Apple has historically been more than half of revenue (Maybank Kim Eng, 2023), and Q4 launch/holiday trading has been about 30% of annual profit. That concentration makes the stock unusually sensitive to a handful of daily-observable catalysts — launch demand and allocation news, Apple price resets, the THB, rates and consumer-credit conditions — and to the market’s expectation path around them.

  • Portfolio management. The shares re-rated about +68% YTD to ~THB 33 by early Nov-2026 (MarketScreener quote, 3 Nov), with a P/E in the high teens, ROE near 39% and D/E near 0.9×. A high-expectation, levered-to-sentiment name warrants daily visibility of what is pushing the price so sizing follows evidence, not narrative.
  • Risk mitigation. Event gaps (launch, results, price resets) and a seasonal run-up that brokers measure at roughly +27% on average between 1 Jul and 15 Oct (2022–26) make the post-window phase — where we are now — the period where a disciplined de-risk rule has the most value. Daily regime probabilities give a pre-agreed trigger rather than a discretionary one.
  • Alpha generation. Seasonality is crowded and well known; the residual edge is in differentiating good from bad years (sell-through vs. allocation, margin quality, financing-book health) and in timing exit/re-entry. The radar isolates which drivers are carrying the move so the analyst can challenge it.

Ethical guardrails

  • Decision support, not advice or execution. No order routing. Human sign-off on every action. Distributing signals to third parties can constitute regulated investment advice in Thailand — involve compliance/legal before any external use.
  • Market integrity. No material non-public information; no scraping that breaches terms of service; licensed data only (SET and vendor redistribution terms apply). Do not publish signals in a way that could be used to move a thinly-traded stock.
  • Suitability & proportionality. A Bull/Bear flag ignores the user’s objectives, horizon and risk capacity; show it with sizing limits, never as a standalone instruction.
  • Conflicts of interest. Disclose any position held by the builder/user in COM7 or competitors; separate research from trading approval.
  • Privacy. Only market data; if alternative data (social posts, app/search traffic) is added, strip personal data and document lawful basis under PDPA.
  • Accountability. Named model owner, independent validator, change log, and a documented kill-switch.

Interface transparency (required disclosures)

  • Persistent “not advice / may be wrong” banner and a SYNTHETIC DATA mark whenever demo data is loaded (both implemented).
  • Data vendor, as-of timestamp, latency and adjustment method (splits/dividends) on screen; stale-data warning when the last bar is old (implemented).
  • Show probability and label, base rate, training window, last refit date, OOS AUC/Brier/calibration (implemented), and the top drivers behind today’s reading (implemented).
  • State what the model cannot see: news after close, halts, corporate actions, liquidity shocks, structural breaks.
  • Past/backtest performance carries no promise of future results; backtests include costs and use only walk-forward predictions.

Validation cadence (SR 11-7-style)

  • Conceptual soundness: independent review of labels, features and thresholds (annual or on change).
  • Ongoing monitoring: daily health card; weekly calibration; monthly drift review.
  • Outcome analysis: quarterly OOS review vs. buy-and-hold and vs. a naïve 50/200-day trend rule.
  • Kill-switch: suspend the label if 60-day rolling Brier skill < 0 or data is stale beyond 1 trading day.
